# Style guide: The Fifth Fret

Principles and policies for F5 essays. Rough; refined as we go.

Related tool: the `bloat-check` skill (`~/.claude/skills/bloat-check/`)
covers verbosity and rhetorical inflation. This guide may elaborate on bloat
but does not replace that skill.

## Signposting

Signposting (also called metadiscourse: writing about the writing) previews
and guides the reader. Some is useful. Too much is a fault, in two forms.

### Hammering the planks

Repeating the argument's planks: preview, argue, recap. The clumsy version is
the high-school five-paragraph essay ("tell 'em what you're gonna tell 'em,
tell 'em, tell 'em what you told 'em"). The subtler version restates each
point more than once across a piece.

Related terms:

- Over-signposting; excessive metadiscourse (Joseph Williams, Style).
- Belaboring the point.
- Over-explaining, hand-holding, condescending to the reader.
- Redundancy.
- Pinker (The Sense of Style) treats excessive signposting as a symptom of
  distrusting the reader.

Overlaps with the Restatement and Throat-clearing items in `bloat-check`.

### Spilling the beans

A summary preview of a strong argument weakens the argument when it arrives
in full at the right moment, like foreshadowing a joke's punchline. This is
a structural problem, not bloat: the words may be few, but they land in the
wrong place.

Related terms:

- Telegraphing (from boxing and comedy). The best fit.
- Tipping your hand.
- Stealing your own thunder.

## Structure and pacing

Rhetoric has better names for the remedies than for the faults:

- Climactic order: arrange points so the strongest comes last.

- Inductive (delayed-thesis) structure: evidence first, conclusion arrived
  at. Contrast deductive (thesis-first) structure.

Thesis-first structure suits reference and technical material, where readers
want the answer up front. Where the payoff is an insight or an argument,
inductive structure and climactic order usually land harder. Spilling the
beans is deductive structure applied where inductive would work better.
